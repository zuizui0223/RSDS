# Pre-q ecological source context: population, time and sex are not interchangeable

**2026-11-15 | Source facts identified before first six-source q results.** This is a population-identity/confounding note, NOT an additional test of original GPS q and not a change to the frozen source roster.

## Barnacle goose: Svalbard and Barents Sea are distinguishable breeding populations, but differ by sex

The 2014 *Journal of Animal Ecology* original study, “Forecasting spring from afar? Timing of migration and predictability of phenology along different migration routes of an avian herbivore” (https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2656.12281), explicitly sampled distinct Greenland, Svalbard and Barents Sea breeding populations. Its published source design reports *Branta leucopsis* GPS/ARGOS tags for 7 Greenland, 21 Svalbard and 12 Barents Sea birds, tracked in 2006–2011, typically **3–7 GPS fixes/day**. Original tagging was **adult males for Svalbard** and **adult females for Barents Sea**. Thus the already observed original full-source Svalbard q (−0.02611, nominal tag CI spanning zero, 6 q-qualified individual tags) and the new Barents Sea original source cannot be treated as two sex-matched replicate populations. Their publication family is **one source publication** with distinct data-package DOI/study IDs, not automatically two independent publications.

At the original fixed RSDS 0.5–2h consecutive GPS step rule, the published 3–7 fixes/day also signals potential observation-cadence failure; q for the new Barents package must be assessed under unchanged support thresholds, without lowering those thresholds to rescue apparent biological sample size. The original publication's reported N=12 Barents tags is NOT the number of q-qualified animals under RSDS.

## Goose farming versus fireworks: study context and original GPS date support differ

The 2022 *Journal of Applied Ecology* study “More grazing, more damage?” (https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2664.14306) analyzed Dutch Fryslân grassland grazing and used GPS data from 57 barnacle and 73 white-fronted geese. In its **published grazing-pressure analysis** it retained hourly points from **10:00–15:00 CET** (November–May), intentionally excluding roosting activity. The frozen RSDS archived source-native q is recomputed from the eligible **original full GPS provider file**, not estimated from this paper's daytime-restricted effect table; these are different measurement analyses. Do not reinterpret paper daytime selection as RSDS night/day estimator.

The 2022 *Conservation Letters* “Wild goose chase” (https://doi.org/10.1111/conl.12927) studied 347 GPS-tracked migratory geese of four species during the New Year period across eight years (2014–2021) to estimate fireworks disturbance. Even with different paper titles, they might draw on partially overlapping tagging programmes or geese; no shared tag identity/nonoverlap verification has been performed. The original q is not itself the fireworks response or grazing pressure.
