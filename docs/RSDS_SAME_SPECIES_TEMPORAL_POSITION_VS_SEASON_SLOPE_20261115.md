## Main observed positive result

**Among six frozen target original-study source slots, three original q source-native effects were estimable**; **all three** have original tagged-animal bootstrap nominal 95% intervals excluding zero in the direction already recorded in all existing supported source-native q archives for that species. The other three original sources are NOT counterexamples: two original-file metadata HOLDs and one GPS source with q unsupported.

**Among those same three q-supported source taxa, two have a new supported q–daylength slope in the opposite sign to at least one older same-species source-specific slope whose original tag-bootstrap interval also excludes zero.** The third fox source has a new q–daylength interval including zero, so no supported seasonal comparison there.

## Biological question now

> Are *temporal niche position* (the sign and relative magnitude of NIGHT versus DAY observed GPS displacement) and *seasonal flexibility* (its within-animal-year change with astronomical daylength) different ecological properties that transfer across populations to different extents?

This question is not answered by a binary species nocturnal/diurnal type. In these selected archives, **all three newly observed source q directions agree with earlier within-species original archives**, yet the source-specific daylength relationship has opposite supported signs for two of them. A coherent hypothesis is that a species can conserve relative diel movement allocation while the environmental or seasonal adjustment varies.

This is an **observational positive result and testable hypothesis**, not established universal flexibility. A reversal of *source-specific nominal within-animal tagged-bootstrap interval signs* is **not** a calibrated CI for the difference between independent source means or slopes; a pooled study-level interaction requires source independence, synchronized seasons and compatible GPS observation support.

## Required boundaries before claiming adaptation

1. **Source independence:** Unique original DOI/study ID does not establish non-overlapping original tracked animals. Wyoming Sublette (2018–2020) and earlier Wyoming mule-deer study (2019–2020) overlap in region and recording years, so actual original tag independence is unverified. Pannonic white-fronted geese and prior original goose archives may also share broader tagging networks.
2. **Ecological context:** Original GPS schedules, habitat, migratory stage, available resource phenology, year and weather differ. Astronomical daylength is not measured irradiance or a manipulation. A between-source opposite q–daylength association need not represent plasticity, evolution or an independent adaptive response.
3. **Prior outcome access:** The fox original GPS had already been accessed for solar-heading analysis (not this q endpoint). The other sources are absent from eight checked prior source output archives, but an exhaustive previous-GPS use audit and original animal ID overlap audit are not complete.
4. **Source selection:** The six target source DOIs were fixed before their own q, but target taxa were selected after reviewing older 63 archives; no global prevalence, independent prospective significance, formal meta-analysis or general cross-species law follows.
5. **Separate negative endpoint:** The old 48-source shortwave–heading *external signed directional effect* was **NOT REPLICATED**; the old 12 ERA5 raw source SHA remain HOLD. These are not rehabilitated by q/daylength results.
